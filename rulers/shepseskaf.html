Old Kingdom

Shepseskaf

4th Dynasty · 2613-2494 BCE

Reign begins2503 BCE
Reign ends2498 BCE
Dynasty span2613-2494 BCE

Background

Shepseskaf succeeded Menkaure and broke with the Fourth Dynasty pyramid tradition by building the large mastaba known as Mastabat al-Fir’aun at South Saqqara. The choice has prompted many explanations—economic constraint, religious change, or a deliberate political statement—but none is demonstrable from the monument alone. His short reign ended the direct Giza pyramid sequence. Archaeological remains establish a substantial royal funerary complex, while written evidence for policies and family relationships is limited. The transition to Userkaf and Dynasty 5 is clearer in king lists than in contemporary narrative sources. Research: Metropolitan Museum; Old Kingdom chronology.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

A break from Giza

Shepseskaf became king around 2500 BCE and ruled for only four years, making his reign the shortest of the Fourth Dynasty [00:15:15]. He notably broke with the tradition of his immediate ancestors by choosing not to be buried at Giza [00:15:33]. Instead of a pyramid, which he either chose not to build or could no longer afford, his final resting place was a large mastaba at Saqqara [00:15:43].

The end of the dynasty

His reign may have been marked by internal friction between the royal family and the increasingly powerful priesthood of Ra [00:15:33]. The transition following his death is murky, heavily involving a highly important royal woman named Khentkaus [00:15:59]. While scholars debate her exact relationship to Shepseskaf—whether she was his mother, his wife, or a regent—ancient king lists universally agree that Shepseskaf was the final ruler of the Fourth Dynasty [00:16:16].

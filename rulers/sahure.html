Sahure

5th Dynasty · 2494-2345 BCE

Reign begins2487 BCE
Reign ends2475 BCE
Dynasty span2494-2345 BCE

Background

Sahure was an early Dynasty 5 king whose pyramid complex at Abusir is notable for extensive carved reliefs. These include scenes of expeditions, foreign peoples, ships, animals and the king’s cult, offering unusually rich evidence for royal representation and resource networks. His sun temple has not been conclusively located, though later sources and architectural patterns indicate continued solar emphasis. The reliefs should be read as royal ideology as well as evidence for activity: they celebrate control, exchange and divine order rather than serving as neutral reports. Sahure’s complex helped establish Abusir as a major royal cemetery. Research: Metropolitan Museum; Abusir archaeology.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Naval fleets and expansion

Sahure succeeded Userkaf and moved the royal burial ground to Abusir, a site that would become the hallmark of the Fifth Dynasty. His reign was marked by significant foreign trade and military expansion. The elaborately carved reliefs in his mortuary complex vividly illustrate his massive naval fleet. These inscriptions detail maritime expeditions to the Levantine coast for cedar wood, trading missions to the mysterious land of Punt, and military campaigns against Libyan tribes in the western desert.

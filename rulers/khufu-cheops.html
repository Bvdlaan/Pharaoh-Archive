Old Kingdom

Khufu (Cheops)

4th Dynasty · 2613-2494 BCE

Reign begins2589 BCE
Reign ends2566 BCE
Dynasty span2613-2494 BCE

Background

Khufu, Sneferu’s son and successor, built the Great Pyramid at Giza, the largest Egyptian pyramid. His reign is illuminated not only by the monument but also by the Wadi el-Jarf papyri, including Merer’s logbook, which records transport work connected with limestone deliveries to Giza. These documents give rare contemporary insight into the logistics of royal construction. Later Greek writers created a harsh portrait of Khufu, but those accounts were composed millennia after his reign and should not outweigh contemporary evidence. His pyramid complex and royal cult demonstrate the reach of Fourth Dynasty kingship. Research: Metropolitan Museum; Wadi el-Jarf papyri.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The Great Pyramid of Giza

Khufu, the son and successor of Sneferu, is perhaps the most famous pharaoh in history—not for his political statecraft, but for the Great Pyramid of Giza [00:06:48]. Standing over 137 meters (450 feet) tall and covering 13 acres at its base, the Great Pyramid consists of approximately 2.3 million individual stone blocks and is the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World still standing [00:08:04].

A reputation of tyranny

While his father was remembered as a kind ruler, later literature, including the writings of the Greek historian Herodotus, portrayed Khufu (known to the Greeks as Cheops) as a harsh tyrant [00:07:40]. Herodotus claimed this reputation stemmed from the brutal labor required to build the Great Pyramid, though the archaeological record does not specifically corroborate this view [00:07:48].

Extravagance and the solar barge

Khufu's reign was a time of immense wealth for the royal court [00:09:07]. Close to the pyramid, archaeologists discovered Khufu's intact solar boat. At over 43 meters long, it is one of the largest and oldest ancient vessels ever found, designed to transport the king into the afterlife [00:08:35]. The extravagance of his reign extended to his family, including his mother Queen Hetepheres, and even his dog Abutu, who received a lavish tomb [00:09:14]. Khufu died in 2566 BCE [00:09:34].

Early Dynastic Period

Den (Dewen)

1st Dynasty · c.3000-2890 BCE

Reign beginsc.2945 BCE
Reign endsc.2945 BCE
Dynasty spanc.3000-2890 BCE

Background

Den, also called Semty, was a First Dynasty king whose Tomb T at Abydos is the first known royal tomb with a stepped entrance. His reign is associated with more systematic and developed use of writing, and his name occurs on sealings and inscriptions at sites including Saqqara, Abu Rawash and Helwan. These widespread records point to an increasingly organised administration. Later reconstructions make Den one of the best-attested early rulers, but exact regnal years and individual campaigns are not securely known. His reign followed the probable regency of Merneith. Research: UCL Digital Egypt.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The double crown

Den enjoyed a remarkably long reign, ruling for roughly 50 years. He was a major innovator in royal tradition. He was the first pharaoh to officially use the "King of Upper and Lower Egypt" title. He is also the earliest king depicted wearing the combined Double Crown, symbolizing total and absolute authority over the entire country.

Architectural advancements

His tomb at Abydos introduced significant architectural advancements. It featured a grand staircase, allowing the tomb to be sealed after the burial, and a floor made of red granite imported from Aswan. He also held the first recorded Sed festival to rejuvenate his royal power.

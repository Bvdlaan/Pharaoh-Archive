Qa'a

1st Dynasty · c.3000-2890 BCE

Reign beginsc.2906 BCE
Reign ends2890 BCE
Dynasty spanc.3000-2890 BCE

Background

Qa’a was the last generally accepted king of Dynasty 1. His Tomb Q at Abydos is among the royal tombs of the period, and seals from the site help document the transition into Dynasty 2. His name also appears on objects from other sites, reflecting continued administrative circulation. The end of his reign and the nature of the following succession are debated, but there is no simple contemporary account of a dynastic collapse. Qa’a’s archaeological record therefore anchors the chronology while leaving the political process open to interpretation. Research: UCL Digital Egypt; Abydos royal-tombs index.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The final king of the first dynasty

Qa'a restored order and ruled for about 33 years. He built the last massive tomb of the First Dynasty at Abydos, which included dozens of subsidiary burials. His death marks the definitive end of the royal line. The transition to the Second Dynasty appears peaceful, as his successor Hotepsekhemwy oversaw his funeral and sealed his tomb.

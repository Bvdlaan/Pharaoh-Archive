Reign begins2475 BCE

Dynasty span2494-2345 BCE

Background

Neferirkare Kakai was a Dynasty 5 king buried at Abusir. His pyramid began as a step structure and was later adapted into a true pyramid, but it remained incomplete. The Abusir Papyri, recovered from the associated mortuary-temple archives, are among the most important sources for the workings of an Old Kingdom royal cult: they record personnel, offerings and administration. His reign also reflects the Dynasty 5 emphasis on solar kingship; his name Kakai appears with the royal title “Son of Ra.” The documentary archive makes him far better understood institutionally than biographically. Research: Metropolitan Museum; Abusir Papyri.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The dual royal names

Neferirkare was the first Egyptian pharaoh known to officially separate his royal titles into two distinct cartouches: a prenomen (throne name) and a nomen (birth name), a tradition that all subsequent pharaohs would follow.

The Abusir Papyri

His pyramid complex at Abusir yielded one of the most important administrative discoveries in Egyptology: the Abusir Papyri. These extensive temple archives provide historians with an incredibly detailed, day-to-day look at the accounting, duty rosters, and religious administration of the Old Kingdom priesthood.

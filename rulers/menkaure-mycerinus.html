Old Kingdom

Menkaure (Mycerinus)

4th Dynasty · 2613-2494 BCE

Reign begins2532 BCE
Reign ends2503 BCE
Dynasty span2613-2494 BCE

Background

Menkaure, the fifth ruler listed for Dynasty 4, built the smallest of the three great royal pyramids at Giza. Its granite casing and the associated valley-temple sculpture programme—including celebrated triads showing the king with deities and regional personifications—make his complex exceptionally important for Old Kingdom art. Work on the pyramid was apparently unfinished at his death and completed in mud brick by his successor, a useful reminder that monument size alone does not measure royal ambition. Later Greek tales are colourful but much later than the contemporary remains. Research: Metropolitan Museum; Giza archaeology.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

Economic decline

Menkaure succeeded his father Khafre around 2530 BCE. Later texts remembered him as a benevolent and generous king [00:13:16]. However, the astronomical costs and decades of manpower required by his predecessors' monuments appear to have depleted the royal treasury [00:13:46]. Because resources were in short supply, Menkaure's pyramid at Giza is only a fraction of the size of Khufu's and Khafre's, standing 65 meters high [00:14:12]. Next to his pyramid sit three smaller structures built for his wives [00:14:29].

A legacy in stone

While his pyramid was smaller, Menkaure surpassed his ancestors in the sheer volume of highly realistic sculptures and portraits commissioned during his reign [00:14:38]. These stunning slate statues often depict the king flanked by deities, showcasing the peak of Old Kingdom artistry [00:14:46]. His original crown prince, Khuenre, died prematurely, leaving the throne to Shepseskaf, a son from a different wife [00:15:06].

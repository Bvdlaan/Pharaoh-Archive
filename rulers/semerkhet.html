Semerkhet

1st Dynasty · c.3000-2890 BCE

Reign beginsc.2915 BCE
Reign endsc.2915 BCE
Dynasty spanc.3000-2890 BCE

Background

Semerkhet succeeded Anedjib late in Dynasty 1. His burial, Tomb U at Abydos, and inscriptions on jars and a bowl from Helwan provide the principal contemporary evidence for him. Older scholarship sometimes portrayed his accession as irregular because of later king-list traditions, but the surviving evidence does not justify a firm narrative of usurpation. His placement in the royal sequence is secure enough, while his length of reign, policies and relationships remain uncertain. Semerkhet is a useful reminder that Early Dynastic history is reconstructed mostly from names, sealings, tombs and later lists rather than chronicles. Research: UCL Digital Egypt.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The usurper

Semerkhet likely took the throne by force. He deliberately erased Anedjib's name from numerous stone vessels and monuments, a classic sign of usurpation and political hostility. His reign was brief, lasting around eight and a half years. The ancient historian Manetho later recorded that his rule was plagued by numerous disasters.

Early Dynastic Period

Djet (Wadji)

1st Dynasty · c.3000-2890 BCE

Reign beginsc.2955 BCE
Reign endsc.2955 BCE
Dynasty spanc.3000-2890 BCE

Background

Djet, whose name reading is itself uncertain, was a First Dynasty king buried in Tomb Z at Abydos. His funerary complex included an enclosure for his cult, and his name appears on sealings and inscriptions at Giza and Saqqara as well as on copper tools from Abydos. A celebrated stela from Abydos displays his serekh, the palace-façade frame used for royal names. The surviving record is archaeological rather than narrative: it confirms a royal cult and bureaucratic connections but does not securely establish family relationships or political achievements. Research: UCL Digital Egypt.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The serpent king

Djet is famous for the stunning artistry of his reign. His royal monument, the Serpent Stela, is considered a masterpiece of Early Dynastic art. It prominently features the Horus falcon perched on a serekh containing a snake, clearly demonstrating the high level of skill achieved by Egyptian stone carvers early in the kingdom's history. His reign appears to have been relatively short.

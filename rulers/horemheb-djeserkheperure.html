New Kingdom

Horemheb (Djeserkheperure)

18th Dynasty · 1550-1295 BCE

Reign begins1323 BCE
Reign ends1295 BCE
Dynasty span1550-1295 BCE
Full profile

The military reformer

Horemheb was a commoner who rose to become the supreme commander of the Egyptian armed forces. After Ay's brief reign, Horemheb finally secured the throne. He believed the religious upheavals of the Amarna period had severely weakened Egypt. He acted ruthlessly to restore strict law and order, issuing the "Great Edict of Horemheb" to aggressively root out government corruption with punishments that included exile and facial mutilation.

Erasing the Amarna period

He held a deep contempt for the Atenist religion and the rulers associated with it. Horemheb systematically dismantled Akhenaten's temples, smashed statues of Nefertiti, and physically chiseled the names of Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, and Ay off public monuments. He removed them entirely from the official king lists, rewriting history to make his reign appear as the direct continuation of Amenhotep III.

Securing the succession

Horemheb ruled for 28 years but failed to produce a male heir. To ensure stability, he appointed his trusted general, Paramessu, to succeed him. Paramessu would take the throne as Ramesses I, officially ending the 18th Dynasty and establishing the 19th Dynasty.

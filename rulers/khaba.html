Background

Khaba is a poorly documented Dynasty 3 king known chiefly by his Horus name on sealings and stone vessels. He is often associated with the Layer Pyramid at Zawyet el-Aryan, but that attribution is not universally secure. No extensive contemporary historical record survives, and neither his personal name nor his position within the dynasty is fully resolved. His entry should therefore be read as a cautious archaeological identification. Khaba nonetheless matters because his scattered attestations reveal continued royal administration and experimentation with monumental construction between the better-known reigns of Djoser and Sneferu. Research: UCL Digital Egypt; UCL research on Dynasty 3.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The Layer Pyramid

Historical records offer almost no details about the events of Khaba's reign. The Turin Canon credits him with a brief seven-year rule. His primary legacy is the construction of the "Layer Pyramid" located at Zawiyet el-Aryan, a few miles south of modern Cairo. Similar to his predecessor Sekhemkhet, the pyramid appears to have never been used for a burial. The location of the king's physical remains is unknown.

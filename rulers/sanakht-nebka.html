Sanakht (Nebka)

3rd Dynasty · 2686-2613 BCE

Reign begins2686 BCE
Reign ends2667 BCE
Dynasty span2686-2613 BCE

Background

Sanakht, often linked with the later name Nebka, is a proposed early Dynasty 3 king, but the identification is not certain. Contemporary evidence for a ruler named Sanakht/Zanakht includes seal impressions and a large mastaba at Bet Khallaf that has been associated with him. The order of Dynasty 3 rulers is contested: some reconstructions place him before Djoser, while others treat the Nebka equation more cautiously. This entry therefore records a plausible royal figure rather than a fully secure biography. The evidence does not justify confident claims about his reign or building programme. Research: UCL Digital Egypt; Metropolitan Museum chronology.

The debate over succession

Ancient king lists, including the Abydos King List and the Turin Canon, record Sanakht (also known as Nebka) as the first sovereign of the Third Dynasty. Modern archaeological evidence strongly disputes this sequence. Physical evidence demonstrates that Djoser presided over the burial of the final Second Dynasty king Khasekhemwy, indicating Djoser was the direct successor. As a result, modern historians place Sanakht later in the dynasty's chronology.

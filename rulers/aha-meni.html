Aha (Meni)

1st Dynasty · c.3000-2890 BCE

Reign beginsc.2990 BCE
Reign endsc.2990 BCE
Dynasty spanc.3000-2890 BCE

Background

Aha was the first or second king of Dynasty 1; like Narmer, he has sometimes been proposed as the historical Menes, but the identification is uncertain. His name is attested at Abydos, Naqada, Saqqara, Helwan and Zawiyet el-Aryan, showing the reach of early royal administration. His royal burial complex at Abydos comprises several chambers (B10, B15 and B19), while sealings from Naqada belong to an early preserved palace-façade tomb. The evidence makes Aha an important figure in the consolidation of the new monarchy, although the details of his reign remain sparse. Research: UCL Digital Egypt.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

Consolidating the kingdom

Aha succeeded Narmer and focused on securing the fragile new state. He established a firm administrative grip over the Nile Delta and launched military campaigns against neighboring Nubia.

The practice of retainer sacrifice

His burial complex in the royal cemetery of Abydos provides some of the earliest clear evidence of retainer sacrifice. Dozens of servants and officials were killed and buried alongside him, intended to serve the king in the afterlife. This brutal practice was characteristic of the First Dynasty but was eventually phased out in later eras.

Queen Merneith (regent)

1st Dynasty · c.3000-2890 BCE

Reign beginsc.2950 BCE
Reign endsc.2950 BCE
Dynasty spanc.3000-2890 BCE

Background

Merneith was a First Dynasty royal woman buried in Tomb Y at Abydos, with a funerary enclosure and subsidiary burials comparable to those of kings. Her name appears on seals and inscriptions alongside rulers, and she is widely understood as having acted as regent for Den, probably during his minority. Whether she should be styled an independently reigning king remains debated; the Sheet’s label “regent” is therefore appropriately cautious. Her burial’s scale and location show that she held exceptional authority in the early state. Research: UCL Digital Egypt; Abydos tomb and enclosure evidence.

Chronology: Shaw, Oxford History of Ancient Egypt (2000)

Full profile

The first female ruler

When Djet died, his heir Den was too young to rule. Queen Merneith stepped in as regent, making her the first recorded female ruler in ancient Egyptian history.

Royal burial honors

She governed with full royal authority. She is the only woman from the Early Dynastic Period to receive a massive, king-sized tomb in the royal cemetery of Abydos, complete with her own subsidiary burials and stone stelae. This confirms she held unprecedented political power and respect.

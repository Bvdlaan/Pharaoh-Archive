Djoser (Netjerikhet)

Reign dates: 2667-2648 BCE

Dynasty span: 3rd Dynasty

## Restoring stability Djoser is universally recognized as the founder of the Old Kingdom. Following the sectarian conflicts and northern revolts that defined the Second Dynasty, Djoser brought immediate stability to Egypt. This unified control over the northern delta region allowed him to launch state-sponsored mining expeditions into the Sinai Peninsula to extract valuable metals and turquoise. ## The first stone pyramid Djoser abandoned the ancestral royal cemetery of Abydos and relocated his mortuary complex to Saqqara, just outside the capital of Memphis. Here his chief architect and vizier, Imhotep, designed the Step Pyramid. Standing nearly 63 meters tall and consisting of six stacked mastabas, it was the first large-scale stone structure in human history. Underneath the pyramid, archaeologists discovered burial chambers for the king and eleven members of his family. ## A lasting legacy The Turin Canon assigns Djoser a relatively short reign of 19 years, yet his legacy endured for millennia. He was one of the few ancient pharaohs whose memory remained highly revered by later generations. When the scribe of the Turin Canon recorded Djoser's name, he broke convention by writing it in red ink instead of black to signify the king's profound historical importance.

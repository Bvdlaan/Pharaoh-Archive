Sneferu (Snefru)

Reign dates: 2613-2589 BCE

Dynasty span: 4th Dynasty

## A new dynasty When King Huni passed away, he did not leave a male heir from his primary queen. Sneferu, the son of a minor wife who was also married to one of Huni's daughters (Hetepheres), ascended to the throne [00:02:27]. Because his mother belonged to a different branch of the royal family, ancient chroniclers placed Sneferu at the beginning of a new line of kings: the Fourth Dynasty [00:02:49]. ## The great pyramid builder Sneferu inherited the previous dynasty's ambition for monumental architecture [00:03:50]. He commissioned or restored at least two, and possibly four, pyramids. He completed the pyramid at Meidum (likely started by Huni) and constructed both the Bent Pyramid and the Red Pyramid at Dahshur [00:04:14]. He is also associated with a smaller pyramid at Seila [00:04:22]. Historians now agree that these massive projects were not built by slaves, but by Egyptian citizens for whom serving the king was a profound honor [00:04:38]. ## Military campaigns and trade Later generations remembered Sneferu as a benevolent ruler and a great warrior [00:04:58]. The Palermo Stone records his military campaigns to the west against Libyan forces and to the south into Nubia, returning with 7,000 prisoners and 200,000 cattle [00:05:14]. He also expanded trade, bringing back 40 ships filled with cedar wood from the Levantine city of Byblos [00:05:38]. In the Sinai Peninsula, rock carvings depict him smiting captives to assert control over the region's valuable copper and turquoise mines [00:06:02]. After a reign of roughly 24 years, he was likely buried in the Red Pyramid at Dahshur, though his body and sarcophagus have never been found [00:06:28].

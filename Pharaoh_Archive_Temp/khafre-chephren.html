Khafre (Chephren)

Reign dates: 2558-2532 BCE

Dynasty span: 4th Dynasty

## The second pyramid of Giza Khafre ascended to the throne in 2558 BCE following the death of his brother Djedefre [00:10:26]. He returned the royal burial site to Giza, constructing the second-largest pyramid. While slightly shorter than Khufu's, Khafre's total funerary complex—which includes the pyramid, a valley temple, a mortuary temple, and a 495-meter megalithic causeway—is significantly more massive [00:11:02]. Huge granite blocks weighing several tons were transported from Aswan, 965 kilometers to the south, to build the temples [00:11:31]. His pyramid is the only one at Giza that still retains a significant portion of its original polished limestone casing at the peak [00:12:09]. ## The Great Sphinx The most famous artistic achievement of Khafre's reign is the Great Sphinx. Carved directly from the bedrock, it stands 20 meters high and 73 meters long, making it the largest royal portrait in the world [00:12:19]. The face of the Sphinx is traditionally believed to depict Khafre, although some modern art historians argue it resembles Khufu [00:12:26]. ## Rise of the sun cult Khafre ruled for an estimated 26 years [00:12:41]. He was the first Egyptian king to adopt the title "Sa-Re" (Son of Ra), reflecting the growing religious and political influence of the sun god's priesthood [00:12:50]. Khafre died in 2532 BCE and was laid to rest in his pyramid, though his burial chamber was looted centuries before its modern rediscovery [00:13:07].

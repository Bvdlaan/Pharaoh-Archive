Herakleopolitan kings, e.g. Khety (Akhtoy)

Reign dates: c.2160 BCE

Dynasty span: 9th/10th Dynasties

## The rise of the nomarchs With the complete breakdown of the Memphite government, provincial governors (nomarchs) began ruling as independent warlords. Operating from massive estates protected by private armies, these nomarchs completely ignored the capital. ## The tyrant of Herakleopolis In Lower Egypt, a powerful warlord emerged from the city of Herakleopolis to establish the 9th Dynasty. His name was Meriibre Khety (also known as Akhtoy). He did not claim the throne through noble lineage, but through sheer force and intimidation. The historian Manetho wrote that Khety was more terrible and cruel than any king before him. According to ancient legend, the gods cursed him for his cruelty, driving him mad before he was ultimately eaten by a crocodile. ## A fractured kingdom The 9th Dynasty—and the subsequent 10th Dynasty, which may have just been a rival faction of the same family—never truly controlled all of Egypt. Powerful southern nomarchs, such as Ankhtifi of Hierakonpolis, paid them only lip service while expanding their own territories. Eventually, the Herakleopolitan kings found themselves in a massive civil war against an upstart dynasty of nomarchs rising from the southern city of Thebes.

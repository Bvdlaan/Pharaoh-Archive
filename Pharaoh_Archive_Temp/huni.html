Huni

Reign dates: 2637-2613 BCE

Dynasty span: 3rd Dynasty

## Paving the way for the Old Kingdom As the final ruler of the Third Dynasty, Huni oversaw a prosperous and highly stable 24-year reign. His territorial control extended from the Mediterranean Sea deep into Nubia, south of the first cataract of the Nile. His extensive building programs laid the direct groundwork for the massive pyramid constructions that would define the subsequent Fourth Dynasty. ## The Meidum pyramid Huni initiated the construction of the pyramid at Meidum, which was later modified by his successor Sneferu. This structure represents the earliest known attempt by Egyptian builders to construct a smooth-sided "true" pyramid. The project suffered from severe engineering errors and eventually underwent a partial structural collapse. Despite this failure, the trial run provided critical architectural knowledge that enabled the construction of the Great Pyramids at Giza. ## A lasting mortuary cult Huni's exact Horus name remains unidentified. Some scholars attempt to link him to "Hor-Qahedjet", a name found on a stela depicting the god Horus embracing a king. This connection remains unproven. Regardless of his specific titles, Huni commanded immense respect long after his death. The Palermo Stone, compiled more than a century later, records ongoing donations to a mortuary cult dedicated to his memory.

Amenhotep IV / Akhenaten

Reign dates: 1352-1336 BCE

Dynasty span: 18th Dynasty

## Pharaoh Akhenaten * **Dynasty:** 18th Dynasty (New Kingdom). * **Title and epithets:** Often referred to as the "heretic pharaoh" for his radical departure from traditional Egyptian polytheism. * **Religion:** Introduced Atenism, centered on the worship of a single deity, the Aten (the sun disk). ## Key figures * **Queen Nefertiti:** Akhenaten's Great Royal Wife. She played a crucial role in his religious transformation and is heavily featured in the tomb's surviving reliefs. * **Meketaten:** Akhenaten and Nefertiti's daughter, who died young and was buried in a dedicated chamber within Akhenaten's royal tomb. ## The royal tomb at Amarna * **Location:** Akhetaten (modern Amarna), Middle Egypt. Unlike traditional royal tombs hidden in the Valley of the Kings, this was located far from the Nile in his newly founded capital. * **Design and purpose:** Designed as a family tomb meant to house Akhenaten, Nefertiti, and their children, breaking from the tradition of solitary royal burials. * **Afterlife beliefs:** Rejected traditional Osiris-based afterlife beliefs, including the weighing of the heart and the Book of the Dead. He believed his spirit would fly out of the tomb to merge with the Aten in the sky and live in a celestial space called the Mansion of the Aten, a heavenly version of Akhetaten where the sun never sets. Despite rejecting traditional beliefs, he did commission shabti figurines, though their exact purpose under Atenism remains debated. * **Surviving art and condition:** The tomb has been heavily flooded and intentionally vandalized. * **Sarcophagus:** Smashed into hundreds of pieces but now reassembled in the Cairo Museum. Uniquely, the four protective goddesses typically on the corners were replaced entirely by figures of Queen Nefertiti. * **Meketaten's chamber:** Features incredibly rare, emotional scenes of Akhenaten and Nefertiti openly weeping and mourning their deceased daughter. * **Burial shafts:** Features deep shafts, the purpose of which is debated. Theories include testing stone quality, deterring grave robbers, or catching floodwater. ## The KV55 mummy controversy * **Discovery:** A fragmented male skeleton was found in tomb KV55 in the Valley of the Kings in 1907. A separate fragmented male body was reportedly found at the Amarna tomb entrance in 1891 but was later lost. * **Identification debate:** DNA and CT scans from 2010 linked the body to Tutankhamun, suggesting it is his father, Akhenaten, aged 35 to 45. However, osteological evidence suggests the individual was much younger, around 20 years old, leading some to argue it is the obscure pharaoh Smenkhkare. The true identity remains contested among experts.

Pepi II (Neferkare)

Reign dates: 2278-2184 BCE

Dynasty span: 6th Dynasty

## The longest reign in history Pepi II ascended the throne at the age of six and ruled for an incredibly long time. While some ancient texts claim he reigned for 94 years (making him a centenarian), many modern Egyptologists believe an ancient transcription error confused the numbers "94" and "64." Even at 64 years, it was one of the longest reigns in human history. ## Isolated in the palace Because he became king as a young child, the country was initially run by a council of regents, likely including his mother. By the time Pepi II came of age, he was accustomed to a life of immense luxury and reliance on others. He grew increasingly isolated in the royal palace, becoming an incompetent ruler easily manipulated by seasoned politicians. Furthermore, the royal family practiced extreme endogamy; his principal wife, Neith, was both his half-sister and his cousin, reflecting a politically isolated monarchy. ## The collapse of the Old Kingdom During the second half of his reign, Egypt was struck by prolonged droughts, low crop yields, and widespread famine. Pepi II's weak, decentralized government was entirely incapable of coordinating a national relief effort. When the frail king finally died, he had outlived many of his capable heirs, leaving the country politically fractured and economically devastated.

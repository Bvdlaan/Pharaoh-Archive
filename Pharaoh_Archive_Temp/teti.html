Teti

Reign dates: 2345-2323 BCE

Dynasty span: 6th Dynasty

## A new dynasty by marriage The transition from the Fifth to the Sixth Dynasty was not marked by violence, but by a break in the direct male bloodline. Because the final king of the Fifth Dynasty, Unas, failed to secure a male heir, the throne passed to his son-in-law, Teti. This shift in the royal lineage led later chroniclers to designate Teti as the founder of the Sixth Dynasty. ## Retaining the old guard Teti's government was essentially a direct continuation of the previous dynasty's administration. He retained many of Unas's powerful officials, including the highly esteemed viziers Mehu and Kagemni. The most powerful man in Egypt during this time was Mereruka, who was not only Teti's vizier but also his son-in-law. Mereruka's massive 33-room mastaba at Saqqara remains one of the greatest archaeological treasures of the era, featuring exquisite reliefs depicting daily Egyptian life. Teti himself was buried in a relatively small pyramid at Saqqara, alongside the smaller pyramids of his wives, Iput (mother of Pepi I) and Khuit. ## A mysterious end The end of Teti's reign is shrouded in mystery. The ancient historian Manetho claimed that Teti was assassinated, though no physical archaeological evidence has ever been found to corroborate this story.

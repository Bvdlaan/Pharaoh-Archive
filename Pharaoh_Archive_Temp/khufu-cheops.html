Khufu (Cheops)

Reign dates: 2589-2566 BCE

Dynasty span: 4th Dynasty

## The Great Pyramid of Giza Khufu, the son and successor of Sneferu, is perhaps the most famous pharaoh in history—not for his political statecraft, but for the Great Pyramid of Giza [00:06:48]. Standing over 137 meters (450 feet) tall and covering 13 acres at its base, the Great Pyramid consists of approximately 2.3 million individual stone blocks and is the only one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World still standing [00:08:04]. ## A reputation of tyranny While his father was remembered as a kind ruler, later literature, including the writings of the Greek historian Herodotus, portrayed Khufu (known to the Greeks as Cheops) as a harsh tyrant [00:07:40]. Herodotus claimed this reputation stemmed from the brutal labor required to build the Great Pyramid, though the archaeological record does not specifically corroborate this view [00:07:48]. ## Extravagance and the solar barge Khufu's reign was a time of immense wealth for the royal court [00:09:07]. Close to the pyramid, archaeologists discovered Khufu's intact solar boat. At over 43 meters long, it is one of the largest and oldest ancient vessels ever found, designed to transport the king into the afterlife [00:08:35]. The extravagance of his reign extended to his family, including his mother Queen Hetepheres, and even his dog Abutu, who received a lavish tomb [00:09:14]. Khufu died in 2566 BCE [00:09:34].

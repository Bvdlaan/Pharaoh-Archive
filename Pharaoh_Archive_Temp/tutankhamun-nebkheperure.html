Tutankhamun (Nebkheperure)

Reign dates: 1336-1327 BCE

Dynasty span: 18th Dynasty

## The boy king Tutankhamun ascended the throne at just nine years old. Born as Tutankhaten ("Living image of the Aten"), he was the son of Akhenaten and an unidentified royal woman known today as the "Younger Lady." Because he was a child without living elder relatives, he was heavily guided by senior officials, particularly his vizier Ay. ## Return to the old gods Under Ay's influence, the young pharaoh abandoned his father's radical religion and moved the court back to Memphis. He changed his name to Tutankhamun to reflect a renewed devotion to the god Amun. He issued a major decree to repair neglected temples, restore the traditional polytheistic priesthoods, and cast new statues of the old gods using electrum and lapis lazuli. ## A frail life and an intact tomb Tutankhamun suffered from severe health issues, including malaria, sickle cell anemia, and a compound fracture in his leg. He died at the age of 19. His enduring global fame stems entirely from the discovery of his tomb in 1922 by Howard Carter. Because the tomb miraculously escaped ancient looters, it contained over 5,000 intact artifacts, including his famous solid gold funerary mask and innermost coffin.

Shoshenq I (Hedjkheperre)

Reign dates: 945-924 BCE

Dynasty span: 22nd Dynasty (Bubastite/Libyan)

## The rise of the Libyans Shoshenq I founded the 22nd Dynasty and fundamentally transformed the political landscape of Egypt. He was the Great Chief of the Ma (Meshwesh), a powerful Libyan tribe that had gradually settled in the Nile Delta over several generations. He leveraged his position as the supreme military commander and his familial connections to the previous 21st Dynasty to legitimize his rise to power. His kingship was officially sanctioned by the oracle of the god Amun in Thebes, marking a peaceful transition of power. ## A unified Egypt Unlike his immediate predecessors who tolerated a divided Egypt, Shoshenq I actively unified the country. He appointed his own sons to key strategic positions: one son became the High Priest of Amun in Thebes (commanding the military in the south), while another commanded the garrison at Herakleopolis. ## The Canaanite campaign To solidify his legacy as a traditional pharaoh, he launched a massive military campaign into Canaan around 925 BCE. He divided his forces to strike multiple targets, successfully capturing over 70 cities and territories, including Megiddo and cities in the Negev desert. Biblical texts equate him with "King Shishak" who supposedly looted Jerusalem, though his own Egyptian inscriptions do not explicitly list Jerusalem among his conquests. He died shortly after this campaign before he could fully re-establish a permanent Egyptian empire in the Levant.

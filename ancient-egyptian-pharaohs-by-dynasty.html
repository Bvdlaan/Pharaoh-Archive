Early Dynastic Period

The Early Dynastic Period saw the consolidation of kingship and central government along the Nile. Its rulers established royal titles, court traditions and burial practices that shaped the pharaonic state.

First Intermediate Period

During the First Intermediate Period, competing regional centres and short-lived royal lines followed the decline of Old Kingdom central power. The surviving chronology remains especially uncertain.

Middle Kingdom

The Middle Kingdom restored a strong central monarchy and expanded Egyptian influence into Nubia. Its rulers combined administrative reform, military activity and an enduring literary and artistic legacy.

Second Intermediate Period

The Second Intermediate Period was marked by divided rule, including Hyksos power in the north and Theban resistance in the south, before Egypt was reunified under the founders of the New Kingdom.

  • Khyan (Seuserenre)

Macedonian (Argead) Period

The Macedonian conquest placed Egypt within the empire of Alexander the Great and the succession struggles that followed his death, preparing the way for Ptolemaic rule.

Macedonian Dynasty

332-305 BCE

  • Alexander III the Great
  • Philip III Arrhidaeus
  • Alexander IV

Ptolemaic Period

The Ptolemaic dynasty fused Macedonian royal power with Egyptian pharaonic traditions. Its rulers governed from Alexandria until the Roman annexation of Egypt after the reign of Cleopatra VII.
